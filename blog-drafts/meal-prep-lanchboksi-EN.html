Thinking every morning about what to take to school or the office wastes time and often ends in an unhealthy choice. This is exactly where meal prep helps. You cook a few portions in one or two evenings, divide them into your lunchbox, and have clean, balanced food ready on your workdays.

There is one key rule here: a prepared meal stays safe in the fridge for 3 to 4 days, so you do not cook the whole week at once, but in two stages. Below you will find 5 recipes, the storage time for each, and two tricks that keep food from spoiling.

What meal prep is and why it is worth it

Meal prep means cooking food in a larger batch and splitting it into portions. It saves time and money, reduces food waste, and helps you avoid fast food. All you really need is a few quality containers and a convenient lunchbox.

Two tricks that keep food fresh

1. Two stage prep. Since a cooked meal only lasts 3 to 4 days, prepare the week in two parts. For example, on Sunday evening make the portions for Monday to Wednesday, and on Wednesday evening make Thursday and Friday. This way the food is always fresh.

2. The salad constructor. Chopped cucumber and tomato release water in salt and oil within 12 to 24 hours, turn soggy, and go soft. So pack only the dry ingredients (chickpeas, pasta, cheese) in the container, and carry the raw vegetables and the dressing (oil, lemon) separately in a small cup, then mix right before eating.

1. Overnight oats with fruit

The simplest recipe, with no cooking at all.

Ingredients (1 portion):

  • 5 tablespoons of rolled oats
  • 150 ml of natural yogurt or milk
  • 1 teaspoon of honey
  • A handful of fruit or berries

How to make it:

  1. Mix the oats, yogurt, and honey in a jar or glass container.
  2. Close it and leave it in the fridge overnight, at least 6 hours.
  3. Add the fruit on the day you eat it so it does not turn sour. It tastes great cold.

Storage: The oats keep in the fridge for up to 3 days. Keep the fruit separately and add it just before eating.

2. Chicken and rice bowl

A filling lunch rich in protein.

Ingredients (2 portions):

  • 1 chicken fillet
  • 1 cup of rice
  • Vegetables: carrot, broccoli, bell pepper
  • Olive oil, salt, pepper

How to make it:

  1. Cook the rice separately.
  2. Cut the chicken fillet into pieces and fry or bake well until fully cooked inside.
  3. Saute the vegetables for a few minutes so they stay crisp.
  4. Pack the rice, chicken, and vegetables into separate compartments of the container.

Storage: Keeps in the fridge for 3 to 4 days. Add a little water before reheating, because rice dries out in the fridge.

3. Chickpea salad

A vegetarian, quick option full of fiber, and ideal for the salad constructor.

Ingredients (2 portions):

  • 1 can of cooked chickpeas
  • 1 cucumber, 2 tomatoes
  • Half a red onion
  • Olive oil, lemon juice, salt

How to make it:

  1. Rinse the chickpeas with water, drain them, and store them in the container.
  2. Cut the cucumber, tomato, and onion on the day you eat, or carry them separately in a small cup.
  3. Carry the oil and lemon separately and dress the salad only right before eating.

Storage: The chickpeas keep for 3 to 4 days. Always store the raw vegetables separately so they do not turn soggy.

4. Cold pasta salad

Loved by both children and adults.

Ingredients (2 portions):

  • 200 g of whole grain pasta
  • Cherry tomatoes, cucumber
  • Feta cheese
  • Olive oil, fresh herbs

How to make it:

  1. Cook the pasta, cool it well, and store it together with the feta.
  2. Carry the cherry tomatoes and cucumber separately.
  3. Add the oil and herbs right before eating, then mix.

Storage: The pasta with feta keeps for 3 days. Store the raw vegetables separately.

5. Egg muffins

A perfect breakfast or lunch addition.

Ingredients (6 pieces):

  • 4 eggs
  • Chopped spinach or bell pepper
  • A little cheese
  • Salt, pepper

How to make it:

  1. Whisk the eggs, add the vegetables and cheese.
  2. Pour into a muffin tray.
  3. Bake at 180 degrees for 18 minutes, until fully set.

Storage: Keeps in the fridge for 3 days, or freeze for up to 2 weeks and reheat in the microwave when needed.

Rules for safe storage

  • A cooked meal stays safe in the fridge for only 3 to 4 days. From the 5th day the risk of bacteria growth rises.
  • Let hot food cool to room temperature first, and only then close the container.
  • Separate the meat and vegetable sections to keep taste and freshness.
  • Use a glass container that does not hold odors and is safe for reheating.
